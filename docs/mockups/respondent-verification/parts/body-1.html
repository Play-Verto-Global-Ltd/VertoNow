Door two — Continue with Google 4 taps

Two taps here could each be called "the extra step", so both are named. Tap 1 is the reveal: the card opens collapsed, so "Continue with Google" is not on screen until "Create my account" is pressed — added deliberately on 14 Sep (d56a8f0) because the open form was 560px of an 844px screen. It is a real cost and it is shared with the password door. Tap 3 is the interstitial, which is unique to Google and is a second button identical to the one just pressed. This deck treats tap 3 as the extra step, because tap 1 buys something measurable and tap 3 buys nothing the respondent can see — but if the brief meant the reveal, §6 is the wrong section and the answer is to surface Google above the fold on the collapsed card, which is a layout change and a different piece of work.

Notes

  1. 1

    The fine print already promises there is no email. "You're signed in the moment you're done — nothing to wait for in your inbox." That sentence is true today and board C makes it false, so it is one of the strings that has to change in the same commit — in all 26 locale files.§5 §7

  2. 2

    Door one has an interstitial too, and it is not the same problem. PlayerSignInsController splits GET from POST because corporate link scanners follow GETs, and a single-use link a scanner can spend is a link the recipient can never use. That reasoning is about emailed links. A signup link is handed straight back in the JSON response and never enters an inbox — so the scanner argument does not cover this screen, and §6 asks whether it should auto-submit. It also carries a copy bug worth a one-line fix while someone is in there: the heading and body switch on proves_address?, but the fine print below them does not — so a signup arrival is told "Links work once, and expire 20 minutes after they're sent", describing an email that was never sent.§6

  3. 3

    The end state of door one is an account nobody can write to. Board B is this fact drawn out. And the account page offers her nothing: /you/account renders the Verified tag when she is verified and renders nothing at all when she is not — no explanation, no resend. Two routes out do exist, and board B shows why neither counts.§2 §4

  4. 4

    This is the screen the brief is about. It is titled "One last tap", which is the app apologising for itself. It exists for a real reason — OmniAuth 2.x makes the request phase a CSRF-protected POST, and /play/:token is inside the Service Worker's scope and can be served from cache with an authenticity token of any age, so the card cannot POST to Google itself. §6 separates that constraint (which is real) from the decision not to auto-submit (which is a UX judgement, and reversible).§6

  5. 5

    Worth saying plainly: the Google door is the better door on every axis except tap count — no password to invent at the end of a survey, and an address that arrives already proved. If only one of the two changes in this deck ships, §6 is the one that compounds, because it moves people onto the door that does not need §1 at all.§6

Neither flow is broken. Door one produces a working, signed-in account that can play, claim and read results — it simply produces one the product may never mail. That is why this is a gap rather than a bug, and why it has survived.
