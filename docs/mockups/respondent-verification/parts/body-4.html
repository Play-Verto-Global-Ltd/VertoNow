The Google door, one tap shorter

The constraint, the contrast, and three ways out

The contrast that makes this worth doing same strategy, same respondent

Both of these buttons start google_player. One of them needs an interstitial and the other does not, and the difference is not the respondent — it is which page the button is standing on.

From the end card /play/:token

Continue with Google

Page is shared-cached server-side (link + deck + locale, 1h) and cached again by the Service Worker.
→ its CSRF token was minted during someone else's request.
→ cannot POST to /auth/google_player. Needs the interstitial.

1

From the sign-in page /you/sign-in

Continue with Google

Outside the worker's scope, rendered fresh per request.
→ token is this browser's, this request's.
→ button_to posts straight out. One tap.

2

And the creator's /session/new

Continue with Google

Same button_to, same reasoning in its own header comment, one tap. The Service Worker's scope was deliberately narrowed to /play/ to keep it off the studio.

Three ways out — and which one to do first 2, then maybe 1, probably never 3

1 · Auto-submit the interstitialsecond

Keep the page, keep the live token, keep the handoff. The form submits itself on first view, so the respondent sees a branded "Taking you to Google…" beat instead of a button that looks exactly like the one they just pressed.

Removes: the tap. Keeps: the page load.
Costs: the Back-button trap the controller's comment names — Back from Google re-GETs this page and would re-fire. Guarded by refusing to auto-submit on a back_forward navigation, which is one small Stimulus controller — the layout already loads importmap through layouts/_head, and sw_register.js already listens on pageshow, so neither the plumbing nor the idiom is new. The button stays in place as the fallback for no-JS and for the guarded case.
Risk: low. No CSRF, OmniAuth or cache change. The button stays; only who presses it moves.
Why second, not first: it removes a tap, but the tap is only felt because of the label (option 2). Fix the label, see whether the step still reads as friction, then decide. The guard is also the part most likely to be got wrong — and the page has a fourth job nobody's docstring mentions: redirect_to you_path if player_signed_in?, which is where the data-loss bug below lives. Its test cost is three new cases rather than a rewrite (§10).

2 · Relabel the second buttonrecommended · do this first

The two buttons render the same I18n string. auth.social_continue draws the button on the card and the button on the interstitial, so a respondent presses "Continue with Google" and lands on a page whose button is also called "Continue with Google". It reads as a no-op because it is labelled as one. Give the second one its own key — a continuation, not a repeat of the choice already made.

Removes: the feeling of a wasted tap, which is what the brief actually reported.
Costs: one new key in 26 locale files, one view line.
Risk: none — no controller, no CSRF, no test rewrite. It is the smallest change in this deck and it addresses the complaint as reported.

3 · Delete the pagelargest

Scope OmniAuth.config.request_validation_phase to accept the single-use PlayerOauthHandoff token for /auth/google_player and delegate to TokenVerifier for everything else. The card then posts once, straight out.

Removes: the tap and the page load.
Costs: a hand-written replacement for a global security hook that also guards creator sign-in, branching on PATH_INFO. The handoff has the right properties for the job — HMAC-SHA256, 32 random bytes, 15-minute expiry, atomic single-use — but the session write that /you/join performs has to move to OmniAuth's origin_param, and the middleware is outside the controller so it can still write a real session.
Risk: high, and concentrated in the one place a mistake is worst. Not recommended as a first move; reachable later if the page load still matters once the tap is gone.

Three live bugs on this path, found while reading it independent of the above

In an embedded Verto, neither door can finishboth paths · verified

A Verto is routinely framed by a third party — the card has its own copy for it (join_embedded, "we can't see what you played elsewhere"), and _embedded() tests window.self !== window.top to decide whether to show it. So the app knows when it is in a frame and adapts. What it does next cannot work there.

after_action :allow_embedding is scoped to show, test_show and live_test_show — the player pages only. The global policy is frame_ancestors :self. But both doors finish by navigating in-frame: _joinDone calls window.location.assign(next), and next is /you/sign-in/:token or /you/join/:token — neither of which allows framing. The third-party parent gets a refused frame where the account was meant to appear. The SameSite=Lax cookie the code already warns about would not have survived the trip either.

So on an embedded Verto nobody can create an account at all, by any route, and test/integration/player_embedding_test.rb contains the word "join" zero times. The fix is to break out of the frame for that one navigation (window.top.location, or target="_blank") rather than to widen the CSP on the account pages — which is the more tempting and much worse option.

The already-signed-in respondent loses the rundata loss · reproduced

PlayerJoinsController#show returns redirect_to you_path if player_signed_in? before it writes session[:player_oauth_handoff_id]. Because the /play HTML is shared-cached it cannot vary on sign-in state, and neither the card nor the JS gates on it — so a respondent who is already signed in, finishes a Verto and taps Continue with Google mints a handoff, is bounced to /you, and the claims for the run they just finished are never applied. The handoff expires unspent 15 minutes later.

Reproduced, not inferred. A throwaway integration test — sign a respondent in with an ORIGIN_EMAIL link, complete a Verto, POST /play/:token/join_google, then GET the URL it hands back — holds all four assertions: the handoff is minted with the run's claims, the respondent is redirected to /you, session[:player_oauth_handoff_id] is never written, consumed_at stays nil, and PlayerClaim count is unchanged. 12 assertions, 0 failures, 1.6s.

The email path does not have this hole: PlayerSignInsController#create applies the claim payload unconditionally. Fix is to spend the handoff and apply the payload before redirecting.

The venue lever does not reach this pageevent-day

#join_google is capped at 15 × PLAYER_JOIN_RATE_LIMIT_SCALE per IP per 5 minutes — the lever added on 14 Sep so a room full of people on one venue NAT can all make accounts. PlayerJoinsController's own gate is 30 per 5 minutes and is not scaled by anything. At SCALE = 1 the first gate binds; at SCALE ≥ 3 the unscaled interstitial becomes the binding constraint on exactly the crowd the lever exists for.

Worse, its refusal is redirect_to you_path with a "too many attempts" alert — a signed-out account page, not a message on the card they were standing on. Fix is one multiplication.

Notes

  1. 1

    The controller's own comment understates its case. It says the page "can be served from cache with an authenticity token of any age", which sounds like a problem for offline respondents only. The stronger fact is in cached_play_page: the HTML is cached as shared bytes keyed on link + deck version + locale, so for nearly every respondent the token in that page was minted during a stranger's request. A direct POST would fail for almost everyone, not for a slow-connection minority — which makes the interstitial more load-bearing than its own docstring claims, and option 3 correspondingly more serious.§6

  2. 2

    This is the fact worth carrying out of the whole board: the extra step is not the price of being a respondent, and not the price of the google_player strategy. It is the price of the button standing on a page that is cached twice over. The respondent's own sign-in page runs the identical strategy in one tap.§6

  3. 3

    And the sharpest version of the complaint is a two-line fix, not an architectural one. _join_block.html.erb and player_joins/show.html.erb both draw their button from t("auth.social_continue") — the same string, twice, seconds apart. The page is not badly placed so much as badly labelled: it announces itself as the thing you just did. That is why option 2 goes first and option 1 waits to see whether anything is left to fix.§6

Rejected without further work: making /play/:token per-session so its token is live. The shared cache exists because rendering that page is a large share of a journey's CPU, and trading it for one tap would be paying in the thing that carries a venue full of respondents. Also rejected: allowing GET on the OmniAuth request phase — the config is global, so it would re-open creator sign-in to the cross-site initiation class that OmniAuth 2.0 closed.
