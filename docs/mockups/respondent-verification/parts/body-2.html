What "unverified" costs: the silent account

The case for §1, in four screens

1 · What the card promised

Keep this
Keep your Vertos
Pick a password and you're in. You'll see your answers next to everyone else's, and hear what happens next.

Four words doing the work: "hear what happens next" is the reason a respondent gives an address rather than just closing the tab. It is the only part of the pitch that needs mail.

2 · What the council can actually send her

…minus this org's opt-outs

Nothing. Not the results, not the follow-up, not the next Verto.

deliverable? applies the same rule one player at a time, so there is no second path that leaks past the scope. The filter is right — an unproven address should never be mailed. The bug is that door one produces unproven addresses and calls it a finished signup.

3 · And the account offers her nothing

/you/account — as it renders today

Email
priya.n@gmail.com

This is where your Vertos are kept.

The template renders a ✓ Verified tag when @player.email_verified? and renders nothing in the unverified case — no tag, no sentence, no resend. From inside the account there is no evidence anything is wrong and no control that would fix it.

3

4 · Two accidental cures

Sign in to your Vertos

Email me a link instead

priya.n@gmail.com
Email me a link

Two routes do exist, and that changes the framing: the capability is there, the discoverability is not. (1) Sign out and ask for an emailed link — that mints ORIGIN_EMAIL, and spending it runs verify_email!. (2) Come back later and tap Continue with Google at the same address: locate_or_create_player! falls back to Player.find_by(email_address:), finds the existing password row, and verify_email! runs unconditionally on it.

Neither is signposted anywhere, and route 1 requires signing out of a working account. So the work is a purpose-built token and a place to tap it — not a missing capability.

Notes

  1. 1

    The three sentences on the card are the creator's to overwrite (Survey#join_body_text falls back to this copy when the field is blank), so the promise varies per Verto — but the default is the one most Vertos ship with, and it promises mail.

  2. 2

    This is the whole business case and it is worth stating without decoration. Every respondent who takes door one is a respondent the creator paid to acquire, holds an account for, and can never contact. It fails silently in both directions: nothing errors, no job retries, the creator's audience count is simply smaller than their claim count and nobody has asked why.§2

  3. 3

    Worth being precise about who is affected. Google respondents are fine — the callback stamps verify_email! because the provider asserted the address, which is strictly better proof than an emailed link. Emailed-link respondents are fine. It is exactly the password door, which is the newest of the three and the one the card leads with.§1

  4. 4

    So there are two defensible fixes and this deck picks one. Either send a confirmation mail (§1) or widen what counts as proof — but the second is not available: a typed address plus a password proves somebody chose a string, not that they can read the inbox, and treating it as proof would mean mailing whatever a stranger typed. §3 works through why the gate stays where it is.§1 §3

Not in scope, and worth saying so: this deck does not propose blocking an unverified respondent from anything they can do today. They keep the account, the claims, the results and the wallet. The only thing verification unlocks is being written to, which is the only thing it is evidence for.

verify_email! is idempotent by construction — it only writes when email_verified_at is nil — so a link re-opened from an inbox a week later must not re-stamp the date or re-send a welcome. The creator controller captures first_confirmation before calling it for exactly this reason; the respondent one copies that.

Notes

  1. 1

    Subject names the organisation, not Playverto — the house rule PlayerSignInMailer already states: the respondent has a relationship with the council that asked them something, and we are the "via". The preheader says what confirming buys, because "confirm your email" on its own is the most ignorable sentence in a inbox.§5

  2. 2

    House mailer chrome, unchanged — #272D4A page, #1C2034 card at 16px radius and 520px, aqua CTA, copy-paste fallback, wordmark. Copy app/mailers/player_sign_in_mailer.rb rather than the creator's mailer: it already carries the two things a respondent mail needs and the creator's does not — I18n.with_locale(player.preferred_locale), because a mailer runs in a Solid Queue job with no Current.locale, and an X-Entity-Ref-ID so a second mail does not stack under the first and get missed.§5

  3. 3

    "You're already signed in" is the sentence that makes this mail honest. Unlike the creator's confirmation, nothing is blocked while it sits unread — she has the account, the claim and the results already. The mail asks for permission to write to her, and says so.§3

  4. 4

    7 days, matching User's :email_confirmation, not the 20 minutes a sign-in link gets. A sign-in link is a bearer credential and is sized like one; this token grants nothing but the right to be mailed, and it is read on whatever day the person next opens their inbox. generates_token_for :email_confirmation on Player, mirroring User — no migration, because email_verified_at already exists on the table.§1

  5. 5

    Landing at /you/confirm/:token, unauthenticated, GET. It is worth noting this deliberately breaks the GET/POST split that PlayerSignInsController uses: this token is idempotent and grants nothing, so a link scanner spending it costs nothing and confirming on GET is what keeps it one tap. That is the same call EmailConfirmationsController#show already made for creators.§1 §6

  6. 6

    No "resend" button on this page and no address echoed into it beyond what the token resolves — a resend that tells you whether an address exists is an enumeration oracle. The resend lives on /you where the person is already signed in as the account in question (board D).§4

What is not drawn here, and matters more than anything that is: the bound on how much of this mail one IP may cause. #join has not sent mail since the emailed-link era, and the per-IP account caps were loosened on 14 September on exactly that grounds. Sending from here re-arms the vector those caps used to guard, so the mail needs its own unscaled counter — §11, and it is the reason this is not a half-day change.

Deliberately absent: a second consent tick. The account already took consent at signup, and asking again on the confirm page would make the mail read as a contract rather than a receipt. Also absent: any attempt to merge this with a sign-in link. They expire differently, prove different things and one of them is a credential.
